# Review 32 — remediation ledger

**What this is:** a finding-by-finding disposition for every item in
`docs/32-claude-opus-code-review.md`, with the code, test, or reasoned decline that answers
it.

**Why it exists.** The review carried 87 confirmed findings, 80 unique after merging the
pairs two finders reported independently. `AGENTS.md §2` and
`docs/implementation-notes.md` cover the headline items, but neither maps the 80 *ids* to
outcomes — and review 26's ledger exists because its completeness audit had to reconstruct
that mapping from scratch and observed that the next auditor would pay the same cost. So it
is written down again. **If you are re-filing a finding from review 32, look for its id
here first.** It may be answered, or deliberately narrowed with a reason, and silently
re-fixing a settled item is its own kind of defect.

**Status of the two documents.** `docs/32-claude-opus-code-review.md` is a frozen record of
the review *as delivered* — it still reads "nothing is fixed yet", because it was written
before this work. This file is the answer to it. The review's **§6a** (10 refuted) and
**§6b** (2 already-known) are cleared candidates and are *not* repeated here; consult them
before filing anything new, exactly as review 26's §6 serves that role.

## The four clusters, and what each fix relocated

The review's thesis — three reviews running now — is that **defects cluster where a rule
lives in prose instead of in a type, a cap, a parser, or a helper.** Each cluster's remedy
is therefore a relocation, and the relocation is the part worth remembering.

**1. The resolver's two §12 directions read disjoint sources.** Capture walked sysfs;
resolution, the ambiguity guard and `enumerate_ports` read only `/dev/serial/by-id`. That
split *was* the bug in three shapes: the duplicate-serial guard counted by-id *link names*
where the hazard is duplicate *devices* — and two clones sharing a serial necessarily
collide on one udev-generated name, so the guard could never fire for the exact hazard §12
and §15.25 promise it closes; a `usb:` identity captured in any tree with `/sys` and no
by-id links could never be resolved back, so `add-node` returned success with a populated
`resolved_path` and the node then waited forever; and the most idiomatic input there is,
`/dev/serial/by-id/usb-…`, degraded all the way to `raw:` carrying a warning about path
instability that is exactly backwards for a by-id path. Both directions now read
`<sys_root>/class/tty` with by-id as a *fast path* over it, ambiguity is counted over
devices, and a symlinked `/dev` path is canonicalized before capture. The in-tree unit
fixture that made the old guard look tested — it invented two by-id names udev cannot
produce for identical devices — was replaced with the single-link shape udev really emits.

**2. Exclusivity was released by exit path, not by ownership.** §15.38's D2 fix put
`set_exclusive(fd, false)` in `SerialNode::teardown`, and there were at least three other
places the node lets go of an exclusive port. One of them is reachable from §7.1's
*documented* initial modem-line assertions pointed at a pty-backed device — socat
`PTY,link=`, QEMU `-serial pty`, the project's own sim — where `set_dtr` fails `ENOTTY`,
the port is dropped still exclusive, and because `TIOCEXCL` clears only at the tty's last
close (which a held master never reaches) the device becomes un-openable by every
unprivileged process on the machine, permanently, surviving teardown and daemon exit. The
claim now belongs to the *port*: `ExclusivePort` returns it on `Drop`, `release_port` is the
one ordered discard, and no exit path has to be remembered.

**3. A parked waiting verb starved its own connection's streams.** `serve_connection`
dropped into an inner two-arm loop that polled only the dispatch future and the request
reader, so the `notes` and `tap_rx` arms went unpolled for the whole wait — measured at
**11.2 MB of console output dropped in one 6-second `lock --wait`**, and a **2.001-second
terminal blackout with 3.3 MB dropped** from a single ordinary contended `send`, because
§17 mandates one daemon connection per browser. It is now one four-lane `select!` in which
dispatch is an optional arm, so there is exactly one place that drains notifications and it
cannot be bypassed. Two siblings shared the root and are fixed with it: `send`'s deadline
now bounds the delivery as well as the acquire (it used to hang forever *holding the
exclusive lock*), and a pty reader parked on a full targetward channel no longer freezes
its own presence, last-close, termios reset and detach-release.

**4. §5's "loss is always visible and attributable" had holes at four boundaries.** A
`faces = target` leg discarded its channels' `DropCounters` at `start`, so ~50 MB could be
shed with every counter in `state` reading zero while an identically-fed `log` accounted for
its stream to the byte; a demux dropped every byte on an unconfigured channel identity with
no counter and no log line; the leg's write half lost the untransmitted tail of an in-flight
chunk uncounted, on *two* exit paths rather than the one the finder saw; and
`delivered_hostward` credited chunks no sink had accepted. All four are closed, and
`SIMP-2` — the structural form of the same defect — is closed with them: the targetward
"charge every non-delivery exit" rule now goes through the `LossCounter` verb at every one
of ~20 sites, with a `#[must_use]` residual type so an uncharged exit is a compiler warning
rather than an audit item.

**And three gates that could not fail.** For a project whose method is "every phase ends
with an adversarial audit", a check that cannot fail is worse than a missing one, because it
is counted as coverage. `p0_license_gate` — the executable proof behind §13's
permissive-only policy — passed vacuously on any `cargo metadata` failure, which the finder
proved by deleting the ban entry and watching it stay green; it now asserts cargo-deny's
`error[banned]` diagnostic and the offending crate name. `p11_replace_atomicity`'s
exclusivity guard, written *this release* so that "a fix that simply stopped taking
`TIOCEXCL` cannot pass", asserted only `pass == false`, which an ordinary echo race produces
just as readily — measured at ~40% detection on an idle box, ~25% under load; it now asserts
the EBUSY *reason* and is deterministic. And the `RefCell` meta-gate's exemption matched on
bare file name, so any future `cell.rs` anywhere in a ban crate was silently exempt; it is
now a repo-relative path with a planted-impostor self-proof.

## Two things a future session should know

**1. Fail-first proof was demanded and, where it was cheap, obtained.** §15.36 made a
regression guard valid only with fail-first proof against the unfixed tree, and this
remediation held itself to that where the shim was affordable: `ITEST-1` was proved with an
`LD_PRELOAD` shim swallowing `TIOCEXCL` (6/6 runs fail, 4 of them at the exact
`{pass:false,received:0,timed_out:true}` verdict the old assertion called green); `TESTR-2`
was proved end-to-end under the offline-runner shape the finding names; `TESTR-7` was proved
twice, once with a planted `#[allow]`ed `RefCell` in a differently-pathed `cell.rs`. Where
the proof was reasoned rather than executed, the guard's doc comment says so. Do not read an
unproved guard as an equal one.

**2. A shared-fixture spec cost a full investigation, and the lesson generalizes.** The new
`HIST-1` browser spec failed in the full suite and passed in isolation. The cause was not
its own: `graph-editor.spec.mjs` attaches a log node to the shared echo console and never
removed it, so the later spec's `discarded_unattached` oracle — "did the device emit?" —
could never move, and read exactly like "the device never spoke". Two fixes, and the second
is the durable one: the editor spec now restores the graph in a `finally`, **and** the
history spec asserts its own precondition (`assertEchoHasNoConsumer`) so the next violation
fails with the right message instead of as a mystery. A spec that mutates a shared fixture
restores it; a spec whose oracle has a precondition asserts it.

## What was narrowed rather than closed

One finding is not fully closed, deliberately, and it is the only one:

**`WEBS-1` (low, security) — PARTIAL.** The per-session bearer token is shell-equivalent (a
holder can `add-node` an exec codec) and cookies are not port-scoped in *either* direction:
the `Origin` check closes the inbound half, but nothing stops the browser *sending* the
cookie to a service on another port of the same host. The exposure is now narrowed in code
rather than only documented — the session cookie carrying the token is `Path=/ws`, the
upgrade route and nothing else, so an ordinary navigation to a sibling port no longer
carries it, and the static assets are authorized by a separate `nexus_assets_<port>`
credential minted from the OS CSPRNG and unrelated to the token by construction. What
remains: a hostile sibling-port service *answering on the path* `/ws` still receives the
token if the operator's browser is made to request it. Closing that entirely means keeping
the token out of a standing cookie altogether, which no browser permits for the WebSocket
handshake without either a custom subprotocol value or a query parameter — and a token in a
URL is a token in logs and history. That trade was declined for now, and the residual is
stated in `docs/security.md` and design §15.29/§17 rather than implied.

Two more dispositions worth naming because they *look* like declines and are not:
**`UIR-1`** and **`DEVR-5`** were classified "(b) or (a)" by the review — record the
omission, or implement the clause. Both were **implemented**, because the design wins on
disagreement and both mechanisms turned out cheap: §17's minimal ANSI subset now exists as
`serialnexusweb/src/assets/ansi.mjs` (a resumable parser, because a sequence split across
two `tap.data` notifications is the ordinary case at serial rates), and §17's blur-close
grace interval now exists, keyed on "the console pane is showing *and* the tab is not
hidden" — because switching to the graph or editor page leaves no console on screen at all
and failed even the charitable reading of "on blur".

## The audit round — what a second pass over our own work found

The remediation was itself put through the §15.34 treatment: six independent agents got the review and
a **frozen** tree, never the implementers' reports, and were told to refute the claim that each finding
was fixed. That is the same rule the review used on its own finders, applied one level up, and it was
worth the cost — it returned **68 ADDRESSED, 11 ADDRESSED_WEAKLY, 2 NOT_ADDRESSED**, plus ten defects
nobody had filed. The uncomfortable part is the useful part: **several were regressions the
remediation itself introduced.** They are recorded here rather than quietly fixed, because a
remediation that can introduce regressions is a fact about the process, not an accident.

- **`SERX-2` — the fix made it worse, and only hardware showed it.** Making the signal restore
  generation-scoped (so a torn-down node cannot drive a *successor's* line) removed the only code that
  ever issues `TIOCCBRK`. A `send-break` straddled by `load --replace` therefore left the tty in break
  **forever**, where it had previously cleared after `ms` — the successor reporting `active`/`open:
  true`, `send` reporting bytes accepted, `driver_counters.tx` climbing, and nothing on the wire. The
  audit reproduced it on the bench crossover rig. What the first fix missed is that **break is tty
  state, not fd state**: it outlives the node exactly the way `TIOCEXCL` does. So the remedy is
  invariant 15 extended rather than a timer restored — `release_claims` gives back *every* tty-level
  assertion the node made, in order, under the same at-most-once flag. Fail-first proved on hardware.
- **`CONC-1` — the fix reintroduced the stale-command hazard.** Holding a payload in a `pending` slot
  (so the reader stops parking its own lifecycle block) put that chunk *outside* the targetward
  pipeline, where `drain_to_quiescence` cannot see it — so an operator's pre-outage console input was
  written to the device the moment it came back, which is §6's "twenty minutes of buffered commands
  must not fire into its boot prompt", verbatim. A second consequence of the same slot: the held chunk
  could be delivered after detach released the lock.
- **`WEB-5` — the fix made the denial four times cheaper.** Splitting the connection pool by taking a
  32-slot pre-auth permit *before reading a byte* meant a full pre-auth pool refused every new
  connection, including one carrying a valid session cookie: 32 sockets instead of 128, and the
  operator structurally the victim.
- **`TESTR-2` — the fix turned vacuously-green into hard-red.** Asserting cargo-deny's `error[banned]`
  diagnostic is right, but the gate then *failed* on any runner that cannot resolve `serialport`. A
  gate needs three outcomes, not two: ban hit, ban list gutted, and **could not evaluate**.
- **`LEGD-2` — the fix over-corrected.** `delivered_hostward -= dropped_full` under-counts, because
  `dropped_full` accumulates *per sink*: a `[Ok, Full]` fan-out credited **zero** delivered for a chunk
  a live consumer had received in full. `FanOut` now reports `delivered` distinctly from `live`, so the
  two questions stop sharing one field.

Eleven more were `ADDRESSED_WEAKLY` — the reproduction closed, a neighbour still open. The two that
mattered most were both "the fix runs on one of two paths": `RES-1`'s ambiguity guard ran only on the
capture path, so two nodes with the same `usb:` identity still both bound `/dev/ttyUSB0` when reached
through `load` or the state file; and `RES-2`'s third operator-facing surface, `nexus-doctor`, still
reported "absent (no USB-serial adapter)" in exactly the environment the finding is about — the
diagnostic AGENTS §3 tells operators to attach to every bug report. All are fixed.

The audit also judged **nineteen guards unable to fail**, which is the highest-yield thing it did,
because a guard that cannot fail is counted as coverage. The pattern is worth naming: most of them
tested *the fix's own shape* rather than the behaviour — a test that drives a function the fix
introduced cannot fail against the code that lacked it. Each was rewritten to drive the real entry
point, and the ones whose fail-first proof was cheap now carry it.

### One failure that was not ours

A single full-suite run failed `p6_outage::outage_faults_then_purges_then_recovers_byte_clean` with
`received: 8190, sent: 4096` — an apparent *doubling*, which is a much narrower clue than "flaky" and
was chased rather than re-run (§15.36). It is neither a doubling nor ours:

- The extra bytes are the **outage-era hostward backlog** (step 2's abandoned 64 KiB burst), released
  across the restored link on reconnect and landing in the pty console the round-trip client is about
  to attach to — a ~20-30 ms window. Byte-provenance settled it: every stale byte matched that burst's
  seed and **zero** matched any other phase's. Releasing it is correct; purge-on-reconnect is
  targetward-only by design (§6's "one sanctioned targetward drain").
- **"8190" is pts geometry, not duplication.** A pts hands out at most 4095 bytes per read
  (`N_TTY_BUF_SIZE - 1`), and `nexus-sim`'s `read_until` loops `while out.len() < n` without capping
  the append at `n` — unlike `recv_loop`, which caps. So *any* contaminated stream reports
  4095 + 4095 = 8190. The clue that looked like duplication was an artefact of the oracle's counting
  rule, which is precisely the harness-honesty class §15.36 legislated against.
- **Not a regression**: the mechanism reproduces on a daemon built from the pre-remediation commit in a
  throwaway worktree (16 380 stale bytes, 21 ms after attach), the code is byte-identical on every path
  in the chain, and the sensitive-probe hit rates are statistically indistinguishable (HEAD 1/22,
  remediated tree 1/20). CPU load *suppresses* it, which is why every loaded re-run was green and is a
  useful counter-example to the usual assumption.

Three fixes came out of it, none of them "retry the test": the sim's `read_until` now caps at its
budget and surfaces an overshoot rather than hiding contamination; `p6_outage` step 7 starts from a
known-quiet console so it measures data-plane recovery instead of attach timing; and the investigation
turned up a **deterministic product defect** on the way — §7.2 promises "every client session starts
deterministic" on last close, but the baseline reset only resets termios and never drains the pts input
queue, so a fresh session inherits the previous session's undelivered bytes while `state` reports
`discarded_no_client: 0`. That one is a §7.2 and §5 violation with a one-command reproduction, and it
predates all of this.
